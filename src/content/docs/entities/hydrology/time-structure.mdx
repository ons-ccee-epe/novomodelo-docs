## Stages, blocks and the end of the horizon

<TimeStructure lang="en" />

## What the model decides

Nothing about the time structure itself: it is data. The model decides how to operate within it, stage by stage and block by block.

## What you provide

- The stages, each with an identifier, a start date and an end date, with the end after the start.
- The blocks of each stage: an identifier, a name and a duration in hours, always above zero.
- The block mode of each stage: parallel (the default) or chronological.
- The number of openings of each stage (see the [Scenarios and openings](/entities/hydrology/scenarios/) page).
- Optionally, the season of each stage, through the season map (monthly, weekly or custom).
- The type of the policy graph, which must be finite horizon, and the annual discount rate, with an optional own rate for a stage.
- Optionally, pre-study stages for the inflow lags, and post-study stages for deliveries after the horizon.

## Parallel and chronological blocks

- **Parallel (the default).** The blocks of a stage are independent. A single water balance spans all the blocks of a hydro plant, and storage changes only from the start to the end of the stage. The LP is smaller, and this mode suits long-term strategic planning.
- **Chronological.** The blocks are sequential, with storage carried from one block to the next, which allows modelling storage cycling within a stage, for example daily cycling within a monthly stage. The LP is larger, and this mode suits short-term planning with storage cycling.

Novomodelo has one level of decomposition within a stage: the blocks. It has no representative-day decomposition. The mode is chosen per stage.

## Finite and cyclic horizon

Only the finite horizon, an acyclic chain with a definite end, is supported. A cyclic horizon, which would close the chain into a loop for long-term studies, is a reserved design, and a case that declares it is rejected at load. When the zero terminal value would distort the policy, a terminal function imported from an upstream study is the supported remedy.

## Seasons

A season is the position of a stage within the yearly cycle (monthly, weekly or custom), and the stages of one season share the parameters of the inflow model. When the season map has no overlapping seasons, a stage without a season takes the one of its start date. Estimating the inflow model from the history requires the season map.

## Stages of different lengths

A multi-resolution study mixes stages of more than one length in a single horizon, for example a monthly head followed by a quarterly tail, or a weekly head followed by a monthly tail. All stages share one set of cuts. Each stage names the season it belongs to, and the lags of the inflow model are the duration-weighted means of the inflows over whole season periods. At the first coarse stage, the lag state is rebuilt from the completed coarse periods.

## Before and after the study

- **Pre-study stages.** Dated periods before the first stage that the inflow model uses for its lags. They carry no blocks and are never solved.
- **Post-study stages.** Calendar segments after the horizon, starting exactly at its end. They are never dispatched and never carry cuts. They give a calendar position to the deliveries of anticipated thermal plants that are decided inside the study and delivered after the horizon.

## Discount rate

An annual discount rate values future costs below present ones. It is global, and a stage can declare its own. The cumulative discount continues over the post-study stages at the global rate.
