## The three steps

## How training learns

SDDP never builds the whole future cost function. It approximates it from below with a growing collection of Benders cuts, each a valid underestimator. Starting with no cuts, each training iteration alternates two passes:

- **Forward pass:** simulates a batch of scenario paths under the current policy, the rule that sets each stage's decisions with the current cuts, and records the trial points.
- **Backward pass:** from the last stage down to the second, solves each stage's subproblem at the trial points, under each of the stage's openings, and aggregates the result into one new cut per trial point for the previous stage.

The loop repeats until the stopping rules are met. The final cuts define the policy, which is then simulated when the study enables the simulation phase.

## Bounds and convergence

At each iteration, Novomodelo tracks two bounds on the optimal (risk-adjusted) cost:

- **Lower bound:** the first stage's risk-adjusted value with the current cuts. It does not decrease as cuts are added and, under the hypotheses of [Tier 1](/math/cut-management/#tier-1-valid-lower-bound), it is a valid lower bound on the optimal cost.
- **Upper bound:** an enumerated forward pass, which visits every scenario path once, gives the policy's exact cost when the risk measure is uniform across stages. A sampled forward pass gives only a statistical estimate, with a confidence interval, that certifies nothing.

The optimality gap is the upper bound minus the lower bound. Training stops when its stopping rules are met: an iteration or time limit, a stalled lower bound or, with an exact upper bound, a gap within tolerance. Under an expectation measure at every stage and the hypotheses of [Tier 2](/math/cut-management/#tier-2-convergence), the lower bound converges to the optimal value with probability 1; under a risk-averse measure that convergence is not established.
