## Purpose

This chapter defines the risk-averse SDDP formulation used in Novomodelo, based on Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR). It covers the CVaR definition, the convex combination risk measure, dual representations, the risk-averse subgradient theorem, modified Bellman equation with discount factor, risk-averse cut generation, and when the bounds are valid under a risk measure.

For notation conventions (index sets, parameters, decision variables, dual variables), see [Notation Conventions](/overview/notation-conventions).

:::note[Symbol conventions]

- $\alpha$ denotes the **CVaR tail fraction**.
- $d$ denotes the **discount factor**. See [Discount Rate Formulation](/math/discount-rate).
- $\mu$ denotes the **risk-adjusted probability measure**.
- $\psi(p, \mu)$ denotes the **dual penalty function** in the general dual representation.

:::

## 1 Motivation

Risk-neutral SDDP minimizes expected cost, which can lead to policies that perform poorly in adverse scenarios. **Risk-averse SDDP** incorporates a coherent risk measure (typically CVaR) to protect against tail risks while maintaining the convexity properties required for valid cut generation.

## 2 Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR)

For a random variable $Z$ representing cost and tail fraction $\alpha \in (0, 1]$:

$$
\text{CVaR}_\alpha(Z) = \min_{\eta \in \mathbb{R}} \left\{ \eta + \frac{1}{\alpha} \mathbb{E}\left[(Z - \eta)^+\right] \right\}
$$

where $(Z - \eta)^+ = \max(0, Z - \eta)$ captures the excess cost above threshold $\eta$.

**Interpretation**: CVaR$_\alpha$ is the expected cost in the worst $\alpha$-fraction of scenarios.

| $\alpha$ | Risk Posture           | Meaning                                     |
| -------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| 1.0      | Risk-neutral           | CVaR$_1$ = $\mathbb{E}[Z]$ (expected value) |
| 0.5      | Moderately risk-averse | Average of worst 50% of outcomes            |
| 0.2      | Risk-averse            | Average of worst 20% of outcomes            |
| 0.05     | Highly risk-averse     | Average of worst 5% of outcomes             |

## 3 Convex Combination Risk Measure

Novomodelo uses a convex combination of expectation and CVaR, the structure of SDDP.jl's EAVaR measure ([Dowson & Kapelevich, 2021](/reference/bibliography/#software-references)), with $\lambda$ weighting the CVaR term:

$$
\rho^{\lambda, \alpha}[Z] = (1 - \lambda) \mathbb{E}[Z] + \lambda \cdot \text{CVaR}_\alpha[Z]
$$

where:

- $\lambda \in [0, 1]$: Risk aversion weight (0 = risk-neutral, 1 = pure CVaR)
- $\alpha \in (0, 1]$: CVaR tail fraction

This is sometimes called the **EAVaR** (Expectation + Average Value-at-Risk) risk measure.

## 4 Dual Representation of Convex Risk Measures

Convex risk measures have a **dual representation** that is essential for computing risk-averse cuts:

$$
\mathbb{F}[Z] = \sup_{\mu \in \mathcal{M}(p)} \mathbb{E}_\mu[Z] - \psi(p, \mu)
$$

where:

- $\mathcal{M}(p) \subseteq \mathcal{P}$ is a convex subset of the probability simplex
- $\psi(p, \mu)$ is a convex penalty function (the minimal penalty)
- $\mathcal{P} = \{p \geq 0 : \sum_{\omega} p_\omega = 1\}$

**Interpretation**: The dual computes the expectation with respect to the **worst** probability vector $\mu$ within the set $\mathcal{M}$, less a penalty term $\psi(p, \mu)$.

### 4.1 CVaR Dual Representation

For CVaR$_\alpha$, the dual representation is:

$$
\text{CVaR}_\alpha[Z] = \sup_{\mu \in \mathcal{M}_\alpha(p)} \mathbb{E}_\mu[Z]
$$

where the risk set $\mathcal{M}_\alpha(p)$ is:

$$
\mathcal{M}_\alpha(p) = \left\{\mu \geq 0 : \sum_\omega \mu_\omega = 1, \; \mu_\omega \leq \frac{p_\omega}{\alpha} \; \forall \omega \right\}
$$

The penalty $\psi(p, \mu) = 0$ for CVaR (no penalty term).

**Interpretation**: CVaR puts more probability weight on the worst outcomes, with each scenario receiving at most $p_\omega / \alpha$ probability mass. For small $\alpha$, only the worst scenarios receive significant weight.

### 4.2 EAVaR Dual Representation

For the convex combination $\rho^{\lambda, \alpha}[Z] = (1-\lambda)\mathbb{E}[Z] + \lambda \cdot \text{CVaR}_\alpha[Z]$:

$$
\mathcal{M}^{EAVaR}(p) = \left\{\mu : \sum_\omega \mu_\omega = 1,\; (1-\lambda)\, p_\omega \leq \mu_\omega \leq (1-\lambda)\, p_\omega + \frac{\lambda\, p_\omega}{\alpha} \;\; \forall \omega \right\}
$$

Equivalently, $\mathcal{M}^{EAVaR}(p)$ is the set of $\mu = (1-\lambda)\, p + \lambda\, q$ with $q \in \mathcal{M}_\alpha(p)$: the expectation contributes the fixed share $(1-\lambda)\, p$, and the CVaR term the share $\lambda\, q$.

## 5 Risk-Averse Subgradient Theorem

The key theorem for computing risk-averse cuts:

:::note[Theorem (Risk-Averse Subgradient)]

Let $V(x, \omega)$ be convex with respect to $x$ for all fixed $\omega \in \Omega$, and let $\beta(\tilde{x}, \omega)$ be a subgradient of $V(x, \omega)$ at $x = \tilde{x}$.

If $\mu^* = \text{argmax}_{\mu \in \mathcal{M}(p)} \mathbb{E}_\mu[V(\tilde{x}, \omega)] - \psi(p, \mu)$, then:

$$
\sum_{\omega \in \Omega} \mu^*_\omega \cdot \beta(\tilde{x}, \omega)
$$

is a subgradient of $\mathbb{F}[V(x, \omega)]$ at $\tilde{x}$.

:::

**Application to Cut Generation**: In SDDP, the subgradients $\beta(\tilde{x}, \omega)$ are the cut coefficients $\beta_t(\omega)$ obtained from LP duals (see [Cut Management §2](/math/cut-management)). The risk-averse cut coefficients are computed by replacing the nominal probabilities $p(\omega)$ with the risk-adjusted probabilities $\mu^*$:

$$
\bar{\beta}_{t-1,h} = \sum_{\omega \in \Omega_t} \mu^*_\omega \cdot \beta_{t,h}(\omega)
$$

where $\mu^*$ is the optimal dual probability vector computed from the scenario costs $\{Q_t(\hat{x}, \omega)\}_{\omega \in \Omega_t}$.

## 6 Risk-Averse Bellman Equation

The risk-averse value function with discount factor $d$ satisfies, for $t = 2, \ldots, T$:

$$
V_t(x_{t-1}) = \rho^{\lambda_{t-1}, \alpha_{t-1}}\left[\min_{x_t, u_t} \left\{ c_t(x_t, u_t) + d_{t \to t+1} \cdot V_{t+1}(x_t) : (x_t, u_t) \in \mathcal{X}_t(x_{t-1}, \omega) \right\}\right]
$$

The measure $\rho_{t-1} = \rho^{\lambda_{t-1}, \alpha_{t-1}}$ of stage $t-1$ aggregates the openings $\omega \in \Omega_t$ of stage $t$. The cuts that approximate $V_t$ are stored at stage $t-1$, and the measure of the stage that owns a cut aggregates the openings of the next stage into it ([Cut Management §3](/math/cut-management#3-single-cut-aggregation)). This indexing has two ends. The last stage's measure $\rho_T$ has no later openings to aggregate, so it enters no value function. The first stage's measure $\rho_1$ also aggregates the first stage's own openings $\omega \in \Omega_1$: the equation holds at $t = 1$ with $\rho^{\lambda_1, \alpha_1}$ as the aggregating measure, and $V_1(x_0)$ is the nested value that the lower bound of §9 bounds from below.

This modifies the standard [Bellman recursion](/math/sddp-algorithm) in two ways:

1. **Risk measure replaces expectation**: $\rho^{\lambda_{t-1}, \alpha_{t-1}}[\cdot]$ replaces $\mathbb{E}[\cdot]$
2. **Discount factor on future cost**: $d_{t \to t+1} \cdot V_{t+1}(x_t)$ discounts the cost-to-go (see [Discount Rate Formulation §2](/math/discount-rate))

:::note[Time consistency]
The risk measure is applied stage-wise (nested formulation), not to the total cost. This guarantees time consistency, which is essential for the dynamic programming decomposition. Formally, with $\rho_t$ acting on the cost of stage $t+1$, the costs of stages $2, \ldots, T$ nest as $\rho_1[\rho_2[\cdots \rho_{T-1}[\cdot]]]$, not $\rho[\text{total cost}]$, and $\rho_1$ also aggregates the first stage's own openings outside that nest.
:::

In the LP subproblem at stage $t$, the future cost variable $\theta$ appears in the objective as $d_{t \to t+1} \cdot \theta$ (see [Discount Rate Formulation §4](/math/discount-rate#4-discount-factor-in-the-stage-subproblem)). Cuts bound $\theta$ (not $d \cdot \theta$), so the discount factor multiplies $\theta$ only in the objective — exactly as in the risk-neutral case.

## 7 Cut Generation with Risk Measures

For each visited state $\hat{x}_{t-1}$, compute the risk-averse cut as follows:

**Step 1: Solve subproblems** for all realizations $\omega \in \Omega_t$:

$$
Q_t(\hat{x}_{t-1}, \omega) = \min_{x_t, u_t} \left\{ c_t(x_t, u_t) + d_{t \to t+1} \cdot \theta_t : \text{constraints} \right\}
$$

Extract dual solutions $\beta_t(\omega)$ and compute per-scenario cut coefficients:

- Intercept: $\beta_{0,t}(\omega) = Q_t(\hat{x}_{t-1}, \omega) - \beta_t(\omega)^\top \hat{x}_{t-1}$
- Coefficients: $\beta_t(\omega)$ — derived from LP duals (see [Cut Management §2](/math/cut-management))

**Step 2: Compute risk-adjusted scenario weights** $\mu^*_\omega$ with the measure of stage $t-1$, the stage that owns the cut (§6), $(\lambda, \alpha) = (\lambda_{t-1}, \alpha_{t-1})$.

Each scenario $\omega$ has a probability upper bound:

$$
\bar{\mu}_\omega = (1 - \lambda) \, p_\omega + \frac{\lambda \, p_\omega}{\alpha}
$$

Since $\bar{\mu}_\omega > p_\omega$ when $\lambda > 0$ and $\alpha < 1$, the total capacity $\sum_\omega \bar{\mu}_\omega > 1$, so not every opening reaches its cap. The weights are built from the tail weights $q^*$, the $\text{CVaR}_\alpha$ weights: the probability vector in $\mathcal{M}_\alpha(p)$ (§4.1) that maximizes the expected opening cost, found greedily:

1. Sort the openings by cost $Q_t(\hat{x}_{t-1}, \omega)$ in **descending** order
2. Walk down the sorted list, giving each opening $q^*_\omega = p_\omega / \alpha$
3. When the cumulative mass reaches 1, the current opening receives the remainder, and every cheaper opening gets $q^*_\omega = 0$

The risk-adjusted weights mix the tail weights with the nominal probabilities:

$$
\mu^* = (1-\lambda)\, p + \lambda\, q^*
$$

$\bar{\mu}_\omega$ is the cap of $\mu^*$: $\mu^*$ attains it, $\mu^*_\omega = \bar{\mu}_\omega$, on every opening that $q^*$ fills fully.

:::note[Special cases]

- **Risk-neutral** ($\lambda = 0$): $\bar{\mu}_\omega = p_\omega$ for all $\omega$, so $\mu^* = p$ and this reduces to the standard aggregation from [Cut Management §3](/math/cut-management).
- **Pure CVaR** ($\lambda = 1$): $\bar{\mu}_\omega = p_\omega / \alpha$, the floor is zero and $\mu^* = q^*$. Only the worst $\alpha$-fraction of scenarios receive weight; all others get $\mu^*_\omega = 0$.
- **Convex combination** ($0 < \lambda < 1$): every opening keeps the floor $(1-\lambda)\, p_\omega$, and the costliest openings, those that $q^*$ fills fully, reach the cap $\bar{\mu}_\omega$.

:::

:::note[Equivalence note]
$\mu^*$ solves the linear program $\max_{\mu \in \mathcal{M}^{EAVaR}(p)} \sum_\omega \mu_\omega\, Q_t(\hat{x}_{t-1}, \omega)$ of §4.2, which has a floor and a cap per opening and one equality. By the equivalent form of §4.2, the program is $(1-\lambda)\, \mathbb{E}[Q_t(\hat{x}_{t-1}, \omega)]$ plus $\lambda$ times the $\text{CVaR}_\alpha$ program over $\mathcal{M}_\alpha(p)$, whose optimum is the greedy $q^*$, so its optimal value is $\rho^{\lambda, \alpha}$ of the opening costs. For example, four equiprobable openings with costs 10, 20, 30 and 40 at $\alpha = \lambda = 0.5$ give $q^* = (0, 0, 0.5, 0.5)$, $\mu^* = (0.125, 0.125, 0.375, 0.375)$ and the value $30$, the mean $25$ mixed with the $\text{CVaR}_\alpha$ of $35$.
:::

<CvarWeightsPlot />

**Step 3: Compute risk-averse cut coefficients** using $\mu^*$ (justified by the theorem in §5):

$$
\bar{\beta}_{0,t-1} = \sum_{\omega \in \Omega_t} \mu^*_\omega \cdot \beta_{0,t}(\omega)
$$

$$
\bar{\beta}_{t-1} = \sum_{\omega \in \Omega_t} \mu^*_\omega \cdot \beta_t(\omega)
$$

**Step 4: Add cut to stage $t-1$**:

$$
\theta_{t-1} \geq \bar{\beta}_{0,t-1} + \bar{\beta}_{t-1}^\top x_{t-1}
$$

:::note[Comparison with risk-neutral aggregation]
The only difference from [Cut Management §3](/math/cut-management) is that the scenario probabilities $p(\omega)$ are replaced by the risk-adjusted weights $\mu^*_\omega$.
:::

## 8 Upper Bound with Risk Measures

Monte Carlo simulation cannot directly estimate the upper bound for CVaR problems because:

1. CVaR is computed over the entire distribution, not sample averages
2. The optimal $\eta$ (VaR threshold) changes with the policy

The exact deterministic upper bound and the `gap` stopping rule that compares it against the lower bound ([Stopping Rules §5](/math/stopping-rules)) require an **enumerated** forward pass together with a risk measure that is **uniform across all stages** — either expectation at every stage, or one CVaR measure (the same risk-aversion weight $\lambda$ and tail fraction $\alpha$) at every stage. Under a uniform CVaR the exact bound is not a probability-weighted average of path costs but a nested, time-consistent risk recursion over the enumerated tree, aggregated with the study's own CVaR weighting so that it brackets the risk-averse lower bound from above (see [Upper Bound Evaluation §2](/math/upper-bound-evaluation)). [Stopping Rules §5](/math/stopping-rules#5-gap-based-stopping-recommended) defines a stage-varying measure and states the setup rejection of a `gap` rule under one.

Under a sampled forward pass Novomodelo computes no exact upper bound. Under an enumerated forward pass with a stage-varying measure it reports the exact probability-weighted path cost, which is not an upper bound on the risk-averse objective. The inner approximation in the appendix of [Upper Bound Evaluation](/math/upper-bound-evaluation) is a reserved design.

## 9 Lower Bound Validity with Risk Measures

Under a coherent stage measure, which is monotone and convex, such as $\rho^{\lambda, \alpha}$ of §3, every aggregated cut lies below the nested risk-adjusted cost-to-go $V_t(x_{t-1})$ of §6. By the theorem of §5, the cut of §7 is a supporting hyperplane, at its trial point, of the measure of stage $t-1$ applied to the opening costs of stage $t$, which is convex in the incoming state. Those opening costs carry the current cuts in place of the next stage's cost-to-go, and those cuts lie below it by induction from the last stage, so the monotonicity of the measure keeps the new cut below $V_t(x_{t-1})$ at every incoming state. The training lower bound $\underline{z}^k$, the first stage's measure of its opening objectives with these cuts in place, is therefore a valid lower bound on the nested risk-adjusted optimal value of the model as trained, whether or not the measure varies across stages, and it does not decrease in $k$, since the problem it is evaluated on keeps every cut once added.

The validity rests on the [Tier 1 hypotheses](/math/cut-management#tier-1-valid-lower-bound) ([Philpott, de Matos & Finardi, 2013](/reference/bibliography/#risk-measures)), among them a cost-to-go convex in the incoming state, which the truncation methods of [Inflow Non-Negativity Solution Methods](/math/inflow-nonnegativity) break in the inflow-lag components; cuts that span every state component the cost-to-go depends on; a non-negative cost-to-go, since the future-cost variable is bounded below by zero; and a fixed terminal function. [Tier 2](/math/cut-management#tier-2-convergence) establishes the convergence of $\underline{z}^k$ to that optimal value for an expectation measure at every stage.

What a risk-averse run lacks under a sampled forward pass is a valid upper-bound estimate. The mean of the sampled path costs estimates the policy's expected cost, not its nested risk-adjusted value; under $\rho^{\lambda, \alpha}$ that value is at least the expected cost, so the sampled mean can fall below $\underline{z}^k$ and certifies nothing. The certificate is the exact nested bound of an enumerated tree under a measure uniform across stages ([Tier 3](/math/cut-management#tier-3-gap-certificate); see [Upper Bound Evaluation](/math/upper-bound-evaluation)).

### Recommendations

| Purpose                    | Method                                                                                                                                                         |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Convergence monitoring** | Bound stalling on $\underline{z}^k$, a lower bound valid under the Tier 1 hypotheses — see [Stopping Rules](/math/stopping-rules)                                                          |
| **Certified optimality**   | The gap rule against the exact nested bound of an enumerated forward pass under a uniform measure — see [Upper Bound Evaluation](/math/upper-bound-evaluation) |
| **Policy evaluation**      | Simulation reports the cost distribution of the policy; it bounds no risk-adjusted value                                                                       |
