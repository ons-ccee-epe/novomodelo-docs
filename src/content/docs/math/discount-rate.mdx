## Purpose

This chapter defines how discount rates are incorporated into the Novomodelo SDDP solver: the discounted Bellman equation, stage-dependent discount factors, effect on the future cost variable $\theta$, cumulative discounting, and the effect on lower/upper bound computation.

For the reserved cyclic-mode formulation (where discounting would be required for convergence), see [Horizon Modes](/math/horizon-modes).

For notation conventions (index sets, parameters, decision variables, dual variables), see [Notation Conventions](/overview/notation-conventions).

## 1 Motivation

The discount factor $d_{t \to t+1} \in (0, 1]$ captures the time value of money, where future costs are valued less than present costs. This is essential for:

1. **Infinite horizon problems**: Ensuring convergence of the value function in cyclic (infinite-horizon) policy graphs, a reserved design (see [Horizon Modes](/math/horizon-modes))
2. **Economic consistency**: Reflecting opportunity cost of capital

## 2 Discounted Bellman Equation

The standard risk-neutral Bellman recursion with discount factor $d_{t \to t+1}$ is:

$$
V_t(x_{t-1}) = \mathbb{E}_{\omega_t}\left[\min_{(x_t, u_t) \in \mathcal{X}_t(x_{t-1}, \omega_t)} \left\{ c_t(x_t, u_t) + d_{t \to t+1} \cdot V_{t+1}(x_t) \right\}\right]
$$

where:

- $c_t(x_t, u_t)$ is the immediate cost at stage $t$
- $V_{t+1}(x_t)$ is the future cost function (cost-to-go)
- $d_{t \to t+1}$ is the discount factor for the transition from stage $t$ to $t+1$

:::note[Formulation Note]
The discount factor $d_{t \to t+1}$ multiplies only the **future cost** $V_{t+1}$, not the immediate cost $c_t$. This is the standard SDDP convention:

- **Immediate cost** $c_t$: Not discounted (incurred "now" at stage $t$)
- **Future cost** $d_{t \to t+1} \cdot V_{t+1}$: Discounted to present value at stage $t$

This is mathematically equivalent to computing every cost at its stage-1 present value, where stage $t$ costs are multiplied by the cumulative factor $d_{1 \to t}$ of §5. The Bellman formulation above is the recursive form that SDDP exploits.
:::

## 3 Stage Discount Factor

Each stage $t$ has an annual rate $r_t$: the study's global rate $r_{annual}$, unless the stage declares its own rate. $\Delta t$ is the duration of stage $t$ in years, its whole number of days divided by 365.25.

$$
d_{t \to t+1} = (1 + r_t)^{-\Delta t}
$$

The rate and the duration are those of the source stage $t$, whose future cost the factor discounts. A zero rate gives $d_{t \to t+1} = 1$.

The rates are set for the study and, optionally, per stage; the Configure tab under [Implementation in Novomodelo](#implementation-in-novomodelo) lists the fields.

## 4 Discount Factor in the Stage Subproblem

The discount factor is applied to the **future cost variable** $\theta$ in the stage $t$ objective, not to the cut coefficients:

$$
Q_t(x_{t-1}, \omega_t) = \min_{x_t, u_t, \theta} \left\{ c_t(x_t, u_t) + d_{t \to t+1} \cdot \theta \right\}
$$

subject to all standard constraints (load balance, hydro balance, etc.) and Benders cuts:

$$
\theta \geq \beta_{0,i} + \sum_{h} \beta^v_{i,h} \cdot v_h + \sum_{h,\ell} \beta^{lag}_{i,h,\ell} \cdot a_{h,\ell} \quad \forall i
$$

The cut coefficients $(\beta_{0,i}, \beta^v_{i,h}, \beta^{lag}_{i,h,\ell})$ are the **undiscounted** values from the backward pass. When the study has in-transit water or anticipated thermals, the cut also carries terms on the in-transit buckets and the commitment-ring slots, undiscounted in the same way. Cuts are stored and managed in undiscounted form — the discount factor appears only in the objective coefficient of $\theta$. See [Cut Management](/math/cut-management) for cut generation and aggregation details.

:::note[Why discount on θ, not on cuts]
Applying the discount factor to the $\theta$ variable rather than scaling each cut individually is simpler and avoids modifying cut coefficients. The mathematical result is identical — if $\theta \geq \beta_0 + \beta^\top x$ and $d_{t \to t+1} \cdot \theta$ appears in the objective, it is equivalent to having $\theta' \geq d_{t \to t+1} \cdot (\beta_0 + \beta^\top x)$ with $\theta'$ in the objective.
:::

## 5 Cumulative Discounting

The cumulative discount factor of stage $t$ carries a cost incurred at stage $t$ to its present value at stage 1:

$$
d_{1 \to t} = \prod_{t'=1}^{t-1} d_{t' \to t'+1}, \qquad d_{1 \to 1} = 1
$$

The present value at stage 1 of a cost $c_t$ incurred at stage $t$ is $d_{1 \to t} \, c_t$.

### Arrow Form and Delivery Discount

For stages $t_1 \le t_2$, the discount from stage $t_2$ back to stage $t_1$ is

$$
d_{t_1 \to t_2} = d_{1 \to t_2} / d_{1 \to t_1}
$$

In this arrow form, $d_{t \to t+1}$ is the one-step factor of §3 and $d_{1 \to t}$ the cumulative factor. A cost incurred at stage $t_2$ and charged in the stage-$t_1$ objective is multiplied by $d_{t_1 \to t_2}$. An anticipated thermal's commitment cost is charged at its decision stage and discounted from its delivery stage in this way ([State Augmentation §5](/math/state-augmentation#objective-contributions)).

### Post-Study Extension

Past the last study stage $T$, the cumulative factor continues over the declared post-study stages, at the global rate $r_{annual}$ on each post-study stage's duration. The horizon is bridged by the last study stage's one-step factor, $d_{1 \to T+1} = d_{1 \to T} \, d_{T \to T+1}$, so the continuation equals the factor a horizon covering the post-study stages would give. [Post-Study Boundary & Chained Studies](/math/post-study-boundary) prices deliveries past the horizon with this extended factor.

### Consistency with the Bellman Recursion

Each stage objective multiplies $\theta$ by its one-step factor $d_{t \to t+1}$ (§4), so a cost entered in the stage-$t$ objective reaches stage 1 multiplied by $d_{1 \to t}$ exactly once. A cost charged at stage $t_1$ for stage $t_2$ reaches stage 1 multiplied by $d_{1 \to t_1} \, d_{t_1 \to t_2} = d_{1 \to t_2}$, the cumulative factor of the stage $t_2$ at which it is incurred. Charging it with $d_{1 \to t_2}$ in the stage-$t_1$ objective would discount it twice: it would reach stage 1 multiplied by $d_{1 \to t_1} \, d_{1 \to t_2}$.

## 6 Lower Bound with Discounting

The lower bound $\underline{z}^k$ at iteration $k$ is the first stage's risk-adjusted value over its openings at the initial state $x_0$ ([Upper Bound Evaluation — Lower bound](/math/upper-bound-evaluation#lower-bound)). In each opening's first-stage problem the future cost variable $\theta_1$ enters the objective with the one-step factor $d_{1 \to 2}$, and each later stage's factor carries its cost to stage 1 once (§4), so the lower bound is stated in stage 1 present value.

## 7 Upper Bound (Simulation) with Discounting

When simulating the policy to estimate the upper bound:

$$
\bar{z}^k = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^{M} \sum_{t=1}^{T} d_{1 \to t} \cdot c_t(\hat{x}_t^{k,m})
$$

Each stage's immediate cost is explicitly discounted to stage 1 present value using the cumulative discount factor.

For stopping rules that use these bounds, see [Stopping Rules](/math/stopping-rules).

## 8 Reporting

Both the lower bound $\underline{z}$ and upper bound $\bar{z}$ represent total expected cost expressed in **present value at stage 1**:

- A cost $c_t$ incurred at stage $t$ enters both bounds as $d_{1 \to t}\, c_t$
- Comparisons between bounds and between iterations are valid because they use consistent discounting
- The simulation output records each stage's immediate cost in that stage's own monetary units (not discounted to stage 1) together with its cumulative factor $d_{1 \to t}$ ([Simulation Output](/reference/output/simulation/#simulationcosts))
